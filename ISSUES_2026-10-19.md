## What changed on 2026-10-19

The published union files sites under 223 UniProt accessions that are no longer current (checked against UniProt on 2026-10-19): 118 are secondary accessions of an entry UniProt merged them into (88 of those entries are also in the set, so the protein appears twice in the explorer), 102 were deleted and 3 were split into two entries. Together they carry 462 of the 13,719 published sites. Example: PRMT5 appears as O14744 (1 site) and as A8MZ91 (2 Larsen 2016 sites, R348 and R488), and A8MZ91 is a secondary accession of O14744.

The 2026-10-17/18 code already moves most of these rows to the reviewed entry through the gene symbol. What it could not do was reach the current entry when the accession is old **and** the symbol is outdated (review issue 7). Now:

- `scripts/00_stage_context_sources.py` stages `uniprot_accession_history.tsv` beside the FASTA. It holds UniProt's `sec_ac.txt` pairs whose primary accession is in the staged FASTA (70,627) and the gene-name synonyms of those entries (22,537). `--skip-accession-history` turns it off. `hpc/ptm_env.sh` names it `PTM_ACCESSION_HISTORY`, and `hpc/slurm_run_integration.sh` passes it to step 02 when it exists.
- `scripts/02_remap_arg_methyl_to_canonical.py --accession-history` uses the file in three ways:
  - **Candidates.** A merged or split accession adds its current entries to the search. A symbol that names no current entry adds the entries that list it as a synonym.
  - **The row's own entry.** A merged accession's current entry counts as the row's own, so it wins ties. A split accession's own entry is the target its gene symbol names. Bare positions are never moved onto the current entry, because its numbering can differ: A8MZ91's source positions are 17 lower than PRMT5's.
  - **Proteome-wide search.** For rows with no candidate at all, the evidence is searched across the whole reference set. A placement is accepted only for a unique site; anchors must be at least 7 residues; the status is `sequence_unique_proteome`.
  - **Output.** New column `source_accession_history` (merged / split).
- `tests/test_remap_edge_cases.py` adds 7 cases; all 21 pass (`python tests/test_remap_edge_cases.py`).

**Measured effect** (2026-10-18 code vs this code, same inputs):
- **Rows:** 0 rows moved or lost; 103 rows gained. Of those, 53 were placed through a merged accession, 35 through a synonym or split target, and 15 by the proteome search.
- **Union:** 13,324 → 13,353 sites (+29, none removed); 4,706 → 4,720 reviewed proteins.
- **Gates:** both pass. The evidence check finds 0 rows placed against or without evidence (the same 22 issue-9 rows stay on the allow-list), and the residue audit finds all 13,353 sites are arginines.
- **Review issue 7's examples now place:** MST4 → STK26, FAM60A → SINHCAF, C11orf67 → AAMDC. So do SARS → SARS1 and UFD1L → UFD1.

### 18. Non-current accessions whose evidence is not in the current entry (medium)

Still unplaced on accessions that are not in the reference set (new code, 2026_03):

| Accession history | Evidence not found in any candidate | Evidence gives no anchor | Multi-R peptide only | No sequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| deleted, or an unreviewed entry with no history link | 179 | 59 | 4 | 1 |
| merged | 59 | 2 | 11 | 0 |

By source: Larsen 2016 176, Musiani 106, PhosphoSitePlus 13, Lim 10, Maron 4, other 2.

Most of the "no anchor" rows are Musiani windows centred on lysine (review issue 3), and are probably right to stay out. The rest carry a window or peptide from an old sequence version or isoform.

*Suggested fix:* fetch the accession's last sequence from UniSave (`https://rest.uniprot.org/unisave/<acc>?format=fasta&versions=<last>`), locate the evidence there (or read the reported position on it for multi-R peptides), and carry the site to the current entry by a local alignment of the flanks. Accept it only when the target residue is an arginine and at least ±7 residues align without a gap. Cache the old sequences with their version numbers so the run is reproducible.

### 20. Evidence check vs step 02 (process)

`qc_remap_evidence_check.py`, for a row whose reported entry is unusable, accepts any entry the evidence matches (`same_gene or cand`). It would therefore pass the paralog placements that step 02 now refuses. It also does not read the accession history, so for a merged accession it relies on the gene symbol.

*Suggested fix:* give the check the same rule as step 02 (a proteome placement only when the row names no current entry) and an `--accession-history` option. Keep the two implementations independent otherwise.

### 21. Release mixing in the accession history (provenance)

Step 00 downloads `sec_ac.txt` from UniProt's `current_release`, while the FASTA may be older (the published run's release is unknown, issue 16). Rows pointing at entries not in the FASTA are dropped, so a later release can only add pairs that point into the FASTA. Still, the history is not tied to the FASTA's release.

*Suggested fix:* when `uniprot_release.json` names the release, fetch `sec_ac.txt` from `previous_releases/release-<X>/knowledgebase/` on the UniProt FTP instead, and record its checksum in `uniprot_release.json`.

### 22. Identical paralogs from split accessions (low; policy)

HSPA1A P08107 was split into P0DMV8 (HSPA1A) and P0DMV9 (HSPA1B), whose sequences are identical (641 aa). TUBA3C Q13748 (P0DPH7/P0DPH8, 450 aa) and PRAMEF15 Q5VWM5 (P0DUQ1/P0DUQ2, 478 aa) are the same: identical sequences in UniProt 2026_03. The gene symbol now picks the entry; without one the row is ambiguous and stays unplaced. A site on such a pair is equally a site of both proteins.

Decide whether to flag these sites (for example a `shared_identical_sequence` column) so per-protein statistics and the explorer do not treat them as specific to one gene.
