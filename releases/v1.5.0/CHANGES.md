# Changes from v1.4.3 to v1.5.0

v1.5.0 adds tables of context marks next to the methylarginine data. **The methylarginine data is unchanged from v1.4.3**: its 100 files are the v1.4.3 files under this release's names, with the data not rebuilt (`release.json`, `carried_over`, gives the v1.4.3 name and MD5 of each), because the build (`hpc/build_release.sh`) needs R with clusterProfiler and DOSE and the network, and no methylarginine input changed. Sites, proteins, records, statistics and layers are those of v1.4.3.

### Files renamed

Every file name carries its release's version, so each of the 100 carried files is renamed `_v1.4.3` to `_v1.5.0` (for example `human_methylarginine_sites_v1.4.3.tsv` is now `human_methylarginine_sites_v1.5.0.tsv`), its content unchanged except where it names its version: `STUDIES.md`, `analyses/MD5SUMS`, `analyses/README.md`, `analyses_with_lower_confidence/MD5SUMS`, `analyses_with_lower_confidence/README.md`, `human_methylarginine_sites_v1.5.0.xlsx`. `supporting/site_changes_v1.4.3_to_v1.5.0.tsv` replaces v1.4.3's (v1.4.2 to v1.4.3); it lists every one of the 11,632 sites as kept, none added or removed, with its records the same.

### CPLM 4.0: license note

The methyllysine table includes all 5,356 validated human lysine methylation sites of CPLM 4.0 (2,939 proteins; Zhang et al., Nucleic Acids Res 50:D451-D459, 2022, doi:10.1093/nar/gkab849; `Methylation.txt` of 2021-06-13). **CPLM states no license**: cplm.biocuckoo.cn carries only a copyright notice ("All Rights Reserved"). The sites are included by the owner's decision of 2026-11-09 (confirmed 2026-11-10), cited, **pending the authors' permission**; the terms the authors give, when they answer, are followed. The CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 of the release does not grant rights in CPLM's records beyond what the authors allow. Each CPLM row has `CPLM` in `sources` and CPLM's own evidence label in `cplm_evidence` (Dat. 2,858, Exp. 618, Exp./Dat. 1,880), so a reader can filter on it or leave CPLM out. CPLM rows are state `me` (CPLM gives no degree); `evidence_class` is experimental for Exp. and Exp./Dat., and unspecified for Dat. (a record CPLM took from another database). `pmids` holds the references that are PubMed IDs (2,919 rows; the rest of CPLM's reference field is identifiers of other databases). Dropped on validation, nothing remapped: 494 accession not in canonical fasta, 60 canonical not K, 5 window mismatch. The ubiquitination table likewise includes all 123,753 validated human lysine ubiquitination sites of CPLM 4.0 (11,839 proteins; `Ubiquitination.txt` of 2021-06-13), by the owner's decision of 2026-11-10 on the same terms as the methylation sites: cited (Zhang et al., Nucleic Acids Res 50:D451-D459, 2022, doi:10.1093/nar/gkab849), **pending the authors' permission**, the authors' terms followed when given. Its rows have `CPLM` in `sources`, state `ub`, and CPLM's evidence label in `cplm_evidence` (Dat. 48,150, Exp. 18,409, Exp./Dat. 57,194); `pmids` holds the PubMed IDs among CPLM's references (123,008 rows). Dropped on validation, nothing remapped: 14,522 accession not in canonical fasta, 906 canonical not K, 86 window mismatch. The sumoylation table likewise includes all 39,493 validated human lysine SUMOylation sites of CPLM 4.0 (6,065 proteins; `Sumoylation.txt` of 2021-06-13), by the owner's decision of 2026-11-10 on the same terms: cited (Zhang et al., Nucleic Acids Res 50:D451-D459, 2022, doi:10.1093/nar/gkab849), **pending the authors' permission**, the authors' terms followed when given. Its rows have `CPLM` in `sources`, state `sumo`, and CPLM's evidence label in `cplm_evidence` (Dat. 2,227, Exp. 29,670, Exp./Dat. 7,596); `pmids` holds the PubMed IDs among CPLM's references (37,854 rows). Dropped on validation, nothing remapped: 13,280 accession not in canonical fasta, 263 canonical not K, 18 window mismatch. CPLM contributes no lysine acylation.

### Berryhill 2023, Ochoa 2020 and the iPTMnet score: license notes

**Berryhill 2023** (Berryhill CA, Hanquier JN, Doud EH, Cordeiro-Spinetti E, Dickson BM, Rothbart SB, Mosley AL. "Global lysine methylome profiling using systematically characterized affinity reagents", Sci Rep 13:377, 2023, doi:10.1038/s41598-022-27175-x). The article is CC BY 4.0; its supplements carry no separate license notice. Its Supplementary Table 5 is included by the owner's decision of 2026-11-10, without waiting for the authors' confirmation, cited. It contributes 5,072 methyllysine rows (states me1, me2, me3 kept; `evidence_class` experimental, as the sites are identified by mass spectrometry in HEK293T or U2OS cells; 4,018 with a site probability) on 2,743 proteins. Of the 6,576 rows of the table, 84 sites were moved to the lysine of their peptide on the canonical sequence and 17 rows were rejected (8 accession not in reference, 3 peptide not in canonical, 6 peptide not unique). The other sheets of the supplements, which carry third-party information, are not used.

**Ochoa 2020** (Ochoa D, Jarnuczak AF, Viéitez C, et al. "The functional landscape of the human phosphoproteome", Nat Biotechnol 38:365-373, 2020, doi:10.1038/s41587-019-0344-3). The article is **not open access** ("© 2019 The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer Nature America, Inc."), and its supplements carry no license of their own. The derived table (accession, position, residue, functional score, best localization probability, best PEP, biological samples, spectral counts) is published here by the owner's decision of 2026-11-10, cited. The CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 of this release does not grant rights in Ochoa et al.'s data beyond what Springer Nature and the authors allow; a reader who needs the phosphorylation table without it removes that source from `sources`, and the rows that only it reports. It contributes 112,405 phosphorylation rows (state `phospho`; `evidence_class` experimental, mass spectrometry) on 10,275 proteins; of its 116,258 sites 3,853 were rejected on the reference check (1,200 accession not in reference, 105 bad position, 2,548 residue mismatch; no window is given, so none was relocated).

**iPTMnet score** (`iptmnet_score`). iPTMnet's download has a `score.txt` (iPTMnet 6.2, https://research.bioinformatics.udel.edu/iptmnet_data/files/current/score.txt); its readme says only "The score is calculated based on all the evidence sources as shown on the website". It is an integer from 0 to 4 and is iPTMnet's own: this release carries it unchanged and does not explain it. `score.txt` scores the site (the rows without an enzyme) under the plain accession and, less often, the accession with the suffix -1 (its isoform 1); the plain accession's score is used. A row has the score when its `sources` include iPTMnet and `score.txt` has a row for the residue: methyllysine 231 of 234; phosphorylation 63,586 of 64,117; lysine acylation 2,657 of 2,672; ubiquitination 609 of 610; sumoylation 33 of 33.

### Ubiquitination and SUMOylation

The two tables hold lysine sites (state `ub` and `sumo`) of UniProt (its cross-link features: the Lys-Gly isopeptide to ubiquitin, or to any SUMO paralog, the paralog not kept), iPTMnet (UBIQUITINATION and SUMOYLATION at K) and CPLM 4.0 (above). UniProt and iPTMnet are openly licensed (CC BY 4.0, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). iPTMnet carries a copy of UniProt, so a residue reported by both may be one record.

### Lysine acylation

The two acylation tables hold the lysine acylation (13 acyl types of UniProt's N6-acyl lysine terms, and iPTMnet's acetylation) of UniProt and iPTMnet, with `acyl_type` in place of `state`; `human_acetylation_sites_v1.5.0.tsv` repeats the acetyl rows because they are over half of them. Both sources are openly licensed (UniProt CC BY 4.0, iPTMnet CC BY-NC-SA 4.0).
