# Changes from v1.4.3 to v1.5.0

v1.5.0 adds tables of context marks next to the methylarginine data. **The methylarginine data is unchanged from v1.4.3**: its 100 files are the v1.4.3 files under this release's names, with the data not rebuilt (`release.json`, `carried_over`, gives the v1.4.3 name and MD5 of each), because the build (`hpc/build_release.sh`) needs R with clusterProfiler and DOSE and the network, and no methylarginine input changed. Sites, proteins, records, statistics and layers are those of v1.4.3.

### Files renamed

Every file name carries its release's version, so each of the 100 carried files is renamed `_v1.4.3` to `_v1.5.0` (for example `human_methylarginine_sites_v1.4.3.tsv` is now `human_methylarginine_sites_v1.5.0.tsv`), its content unchanged except where it names its version: `STUDIES.md`, `analyses/MD5SUMS`, `analyses/README.md`, `analyses_with_lower_confidence/MD5SUMS`, `analyses_with_lower_confidence/README.md`, `human_methylarginine_sites_v1.5.0.xlsx`. `supporting/site_changes_v1.4.3_to_v1.5.0.tsv` replaces v1.4.3's (v1.4.2 to v1.4.3); it lists every one of the 11,632 sites as kept, none added or removed, with its records the same.

### CPLM 4.0: license note

The methyllysine table includes all 5,356 validated human lysine methylation sites of CPLM 4.0 (2,939 proteins; Zhang et al., Nucleic Acids Res 50:D451-D459, 2022, doi:10.1093/nar/gkab849; `Methylation.txt` of 2021-06-13). **CPLM states no license**: cplm.biocuckoo.cn carries only a copyright notice ("All Rights Reserved"). The sites are included, cited. The CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 of the release does not grant rights in CPLM's records beyond what the authors allow. Each CPLM row has `CPLM` in `sources` and CPLM's own evidence label in `cplm_evidence` (Dat. 2,858, Exp. 618, Exp./Dat. 1,880), so a reader can filter on it or leave CPLM out. CPLM rows are state `me` (CPLM gives no degree); `evidence_class` is experimental for Exp. and Exp./Dat., and unspecified for Dat. (a record CPLM took from another database). `pmids` holds the references that are PubMed IDs (2,919 rows; the rest of CPLM's reference field is identifiers of other databases). Dropped on validation, nothing remapped: 494 accession not in canonical fasta, 60 canonical not K, 5 window mismatch. The ubiquitination table likewise includes all 123,753 validated human lysine ubiquitination sites of CPLM 4.0 (11,839 proteins; `Ubiquitination.txt` of 2021-06-13), cited (Zhang et al., Nucleic Acids Res 50:D451-D459, 2022, doi:10.1093/nar/gkab849). Its rows have `CPLM` in `sources`, state `ub`, and CPLM's evidence label in `cplm_evidence` (Dat. 48,150, Exp. 18,409, Exp./Dat. 57,194); `pmids` holds the PubMed IDs among CPLM's references (123,008 rows). Dropped on validation, nothing remapped: 14,522 accession not in canonical fasta, 906 canonical not K, 86 window mismatch. The sumoylation table likewise includes all 39,493 validated human lysine SUMOylation sites of CPLM 4.0 (6,065 proteins; `Sumoylation.txt` of 2021-06-13), cited (Zhang et al., Nucleic Acids Res 50:D451-D459, 2022, doi:10.1093/nar/gkab849). Its rows have `CPLM` in `sources`, state `sumo`, and CPLM's evidence label in `cplm_evidence` (Dat. 2,227, Exp. 29,670, Exp./Dat. 7,596); `pmids` holds the PubMed IDs among CPLM's references (37,854 rows). Dropped on validation, nothing remapped: 13,280 accession not in canonical fasta, 263 canonical not K, 18 window mismatch. 
The acylation tables include the validated human lysine acylation sites of CPLM 4.0, one download per acyl type (14 types; the `.txt` of each is dated 2021-06-13; Zhang et al., Nucleic Acids Res 50:D451-D459, 2022, doi:10.1093/nar/gkab849). CPLM states no license (above). Each download is validated against the reference sequence as the other CPLM tables are (the residue is a lysine of CPLM's sequence and of the canonical sequence at the same accession and position, with the sequence or its +/-7 residue window agreeing); nothing is remapped. CPLM names are mapped to the `acyl_type` of the other sources (acetylation to acetyl, lactylation to lactoyl, 2-hydroxyisobutyrylation to 2-hydroxyisobutyryl, and so on); biotinyl, lipoyl and phosphoglyceryl are new types, which UniProt's terms in this release do not have. Rows have `CPLM` in `sources` and CPLM's label in `cplm_evidence`. CPLM's formylation, HMGylation, MGcylation and MGylation downloads hold no human rows, and its carboxylation, carboxyethylation, carboxymethylation and glycation downloads are not acylation; none is used.

### Berryhill 2023, Ochoa 2020, Hendriks 2017, Akimov 2018 and the iPTMnet score: license notes

**Berryhill 2023** (Berryhill CA, Hanquier JN, Doud EH, Cordeiro-Spinetti E, Dickson BM, Rothbart SB, Mosley AL. "Global lysine methylome profiling using systematically characterized affinity reagents", Sci Rep 13:377, 2023, doi:10.1038/s41598-022-27175-x). The article is CC BY 4.0; its supplements carry no separate license notice. Its Supplementary Table 5 is included, cited. It contributes 5,072 methyllysine rows (states me1, me2, me3 kept; `evidence_class` experimental, as the sites are identified by mass spectrometry in HEK293T or U2OS cells; 4,018 with a site probability) on 2,743 proteins. Of the 6,576 rows of the table, 84 sites were moved to the lysine of their peptide on the canonical sequence and 17 rows were rejected (8 accession not in reference, 3 peptide not in canonical, 6 peptide not unique). The other sheets of the supplements, which carry third-party information, are not used.

**Ochoa 2020** (Ochoa D, Jarnuczak AF, Viéitez C, et al. "The functional landscape of the human phosphoproteome", Nat Biotechnol 38:365-373, 2020, doi:10.1038/s41587-019-0344-3). The article is **not open access** ("© 2019 The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer Nature America, Inc."), and its supplements carry no license of their own. The derived table (accession, position, residue, functional score, best localization probability, best PEP, biological samples, spectral counts) is published here, cited. The CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 of this release does not grant rights in Ochoa et al.'s data beyond what Springer Nature and the authors allow; a reader who needs the phosphorylation table without it removes that source from `sources`, and the rows that only it reports. It contributes 112,405 phosphorylation rows (state `phospho`; `evidence_class` experimental, mass spectrometry) on 10,275 proteins; of its 116,258 sites 3,853 were rejected on the reference check (1,200 accession not in reference, 105 bad position, 2,548 residue mismatch; no window is given, so none was relocated).

**Hendriks 2017** (Hendriks IA, Lyon D, Young C, Jensen LJ, Vertegaal ACO, Nielsen ML. "Site-specific mapping of the human SUMO proteome reveals co-modification with phosphorylation", Nat Struct Mol Biol 24:325-336, 2017, doi:10.1038/nsmb.3366; PRIDE PXD005296). The article is not open access (copyright "2017 Springer Nature America, Inc."), and its supplementary table carries no notice of its own. Its Supplementary Table 1 (all SUMO-2 acceptor lysines identified in HeLa and U2OS cells, as filtered by the paper) is included, cited, with the localization probability (`hendriks_localization_probability`) and the cell lines with an MS/MS detection (`hendriks_cell_lines`). Of the 40,765 sites of the table, 39,935 were placed on the canonical sequence of the reference (38,955 kept, 529 relocated, 451 secondary accession; kept: a protein of the site's protein group is a canonical entry with the lysine at the given position and the +/-7 residue window agreeing; secondary accession: the same after the current accession replaces a merged one; relocated: the window occurs once in the canonical sequence of a protein of the group, mostly sites given on an isoform) and 830 were left out (329 no canonical accession, 496 window not in canonical, 5 window not unique); 77 placed sites fell on a lysine another of its sites also gives. It contributes 39,858 sumoylation rows (state `sumo`; `evidence_class` experimental, mass spectrometry) on 6,313 proteins: 30,898 residues that other sources also report and 8,960 that only it reports. Of the 30,858 it shares with CPLM, 28,312 have the paper's PubMed ID (28112733) in `pmids`, so they may rest on the same experiment.

**Akimov 2018** (Akimov V, Barrio-Hernandez I, Hansen SVF, et al. "UbiSite approach for comprehensive mapping of lysine and N-terminal ubiquitination sites", Nat Struct Mol Biol 25:631-640, 2018, doi:10.1038/s41594-018-0084-y). The article is not open access (copyright "2018 The Author(s)"), and its supplementary table carries no notice of its own. Its Supplementary Table 1 (the lysine ubiquitination sites of the UbiSite-enriched ubiquitinomes of Hep2 and Jurkat cells, both digests combined, as filtered by the paper) is included, cited, with the best localization probability (`akimov_localization_probability`) and the cell lines in which the site has an intensity value (`akimov_cell_lines`). The N-terminal ubiquitination sites of the paper (its Supplementary Table 6) are not included: the table holds lysine sites only. Of the 63,489 sites of the table, 62,951 were placed on the canonical sequence of the reference (61,611 kept, 1,332 relocated, 8 secondary accession; kept: a protein of the site's protein group is a canonical entry with the lysine at the given position and the +/-7 residue window agreeing; secondary accession: the same after the current accession replaces a merged one; relocated: the window occurs once in the canonical sequence of a protein of the group, mostly sites given on an isoform) and 538 were left out (3 bad window, 36 no canonical accession, 495 window not in canonical, 4 window not unique); 80 placed sites fell on a lysine another of its sites also gives. It contributes 62,871 ubiquitination rows (state `ub`; `evidence_class` experimental, mass spectrometry) on 8,927 proteins: 61,032 residues that other sources also report and 1,839 that only it reports. None of the 61,028 it shares with CPLM has the paper's PubMed ID (29967540) in `pmids`.

Rows reported by CPLM alone and by other sources: ubiquitination 125,771 rows, of which 62,291 are reported by CPLM alone and 63,480 by at least one other source; sumoylation 48,593 rows, of which 8,344 are reported by CPLM alone and 40,249 by at least one other source.

**iPTMnet score** (`iptmnet_score`). iPTMnet's download has a `score.txt` (iPTMnet 6.2, https://research.bioinformatics.udel.edu/iptmnet_data/files/current/score.txt); its readme says only "The score is calculated based on all the evidence sources as shown on the website". It is an integer from 0 to 4 and is iPTMnet's own: this release carries it unchanged and does not explain it. `score.txt` scores the site (the rows without an enzyme) under the plain accession and, less often, the accession with the suffix -1 (its isoform 1); the plain accession's score is used. A row has the score when its `sources` include iPTMnet and `score.txt` has a row for the residue: methyllysine 231 of 234; phosphorylation 63,586 of 64,117; lysine acylation 2,657 of 2,672; ubiquitination 609 of 610; sumoylation 33 of 33.

### Ubiquitination and SUMOylation

The two tables hold lysine sites (state `ub` and `sumo`) of UniProt (its cross-link features: the Lys-Gly isopeptide to ubiquitin, or to any SUMO paralog, the paralog not kept), iPTMnet (UBIQUITINATION and SUMOYLATION at K) and CPLM 4.0 (above), with two primary mass-spectrometry studies as sources of their own: Akimov 2018 (UbiSite, ubiquitination) and Hendriks 2017 (SUMO-2, sumoylation), above. A residue that one of these studies and CPLM both report keeps both in `sources`. UniProt and iPTMnet are openly licensed (CC BY 4.0, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). iPTMnet carries a copy of UniProt, so a residue reported by both may be one record.

### Lysine acylation

The two acylation tables hold the lysine acylation (13 acyl types of UniProt's N6-acyl lysine terms, iPTMnet's acetylation, and the 14 acylation downloads of CPLM 4.0 with human sites: 11 of the 13 types of UniProt and iPTMnet, which have the same acyl-type names, and biotinyl, lipoyl and phosphoglyceryl) with `acyl_type` in place of `state`; `human_acetylation_sites_v1.5.0.tsv` repeats the acetyl rows because they are over half of them. UniProt (CC BY 4.0) and iPTMnet (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) are openly licensed; CPLM states no license (above). Each acyl type is its own set of rows; CPLM's types are validated and counted type by type.
